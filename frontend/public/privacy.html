Privacy policy

This site is run by , who is responsible for the processing described here. The operator of this site is responsible for the processing described here. Contact about privacy: .

In short:

  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics and no advertising.
  • Your IP address is used only in memory, to limit request rates, and is never logged or stored by this service.
  • The map shows information that Meshtastic radios broadcast publicly and that reached a public MQTT broker. It is kept for a limited time (six months by default).

When you visit this site

Your connection. Like any website, this one receives your IP address and the details of each request (address, time, browser). This service keeps IP addresses only in memory, to stop any one visitor from overloading it, and forgets each address 10 minutes after its last request. It writes no access logs and stores no addresses. The server's hosting provider or reverse proxy may keep its own short-term connection logs for security.

On your device. The map stores, in your browser only:

  • your display settings (map layers, theme and interface size), where you left the map (its centre and zoom), the width of the side panel and of the packet log's columns, and the nodes you watch (and whether you asked to be notified about them), in local storage;
  • a copy of the mesh data it has downloaded, in IndexedDB, so it loads faster and works offline;
  • the app's own files, in the service worker cache.

None of this is sent back to the server. Notifications about watched nodes are made by the open page itself, from the data it already has; no push service is involved. You can delete it at any time with Settings → Reset and reload on the map, or by clearing this site's data in your browser. The site sets no cookies.

Other services. Map tiles, fonts and styles all come from this server: visiting the map makes no requests to anyone else. Map tiles are loaded from , which receives your IP address and the map areas you view, under its own privacy policy. Fonts and styles come from this server. Weather shown beside routers' readings comes from MET Norway: this server fetches it for a router's map grid cell, so neither your IP address nor which nodes you look at is passed on. Terrain cross sections of links come from Kartverket: this server fetches the heights along a link, so your IP address is not passed on. Place names come from Kartverket's place-name register, which this server keeps a copy of: what you search for is not passed on. Links to GitHub, OpenStreetMap and OpenMapTiles only contact those sites if you follow them.

Mesh network data

Where it comes from. Meshtastic nodes broadcast packets over LoRa radio. Gateways that hear them may forward ("uplink") them to MQTT brokers, and this service subscribes to such brokers. It receives nothing that was not already broadcast over the air and published to the broker. Anyone in radio range, or subscribed to the same broker, can receive the same information.

What is kept and shown. For each packet a gateway received:

  • the node ID of the sender, the gateway and, for directed packets, the recipient;
  • what the node announces about itself: name, short name, hardware model, role, public key and any status text it broadcasts;
  • positions the node broadcast, at the precision it chose, and its map reports (including region and radio preset);
  • device telemetry (battery level, voltage, channel and airtime utilisation, uptime) and sensor readings it broadcasts (temperature, humidity, air pressure);
  • lists of neighbouring nodes, and routes recorded by traceroutes;
  • reception details: time, signal strength (SNR and RSSI), hop counts, channel name and the MQTT topic it arrived on;
  • whether the packet's signature could be verified;
  • whether a directed packet asked for an acknowledgement, and the acknowledgements and delivery errors nodes send back, which show that one node received another's packet;
  • for store-and-forward routers, which kind of message they sent (heartbeat, history, replay) and its size.

Estimated positions. For nodes that do not broadcast a position, the map may show a coarse estimated area, worked out from the positioned nodes that heard them directly. It is drawn as an outlined marker (with an uncertainty circle when selected, or when position accuracy is shown) and is never presented as an exact position. The operator can exclude individual nodes from estimates.

Messages. The contents of text messages are never decoded for display, never shown and never served by the API. The database does keep each packet exactly as it was received, so it can be decoded again after software updates. For a text message, that is the message encrypted with its channel's key, which anyone who has the key can read; the key of the default public channels is publicly known. If a gateway uplinked the packet already decrypted, the stored packet contains the message text itself. The same applies to messages a store-and-forward router replays. Packets whose signature or contents fail verification are kept with their header only, never their contents, and are not used on the map. Only the operator has access to these stored packets.

Encrypted direct messages and private channels. Direct messages encrypted end to end between two nodes cannot be read by this service, and their contents are never stored, not even in encrypted form. Their unencrypted header is kept and shown like any other packet's: sender, recipient, packet ID, size, hop counts and reception details. It shows that one node sent a message to another, and when, but not what it said. The same applies to packets on channels whose key this service does not have: only their header, including the channel name, is kept.

Nodes that did not allow MQTT uplink. Meshtastic lets a node mark its packets as not OK to forward to MQTT. When such packets still reach this service through another gateway, the names in their announcements (NodeInfo) and any status text are removed before anything is stored; hardware model, role and public key are kept. The node appears only as an unnamed node marked with a red ring. Gateways that forward their own traffic have chosen to do so and are treated normally.

Who can see it. The map and its API are public. Everything above, except stored packets, can be viewed by anyone visiting the site, for any time window within the retention period, along with daily, weekly and monthly totals (nodes, gateways, packets) over the whole retention period, for the whole mesh and for each node (hours heard, packets, how many gateways heard them, hours as a gateway, lowest battery level).

How long it is kept. Packets older than the retention period (six months by default) are deleted automatically every day.

Legal basis and your rights

Information about a radio node, such as its name or position, can relate to the person who runs it. Where it does, it is processed on the basis of legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR): documenting and understanding the coverage and health of a public mesh network, using only information its participants chose to broadcast publicly. Measures limiting the impact include respecting the OK-to-MQTT flag, keeping positions at the precision broadcast, showing estimates only as coarse areas, never displaying messages, and automatic deletion.

You may ask for access to, correction or deletion of data about your node, or object to its processing, for example to have it excluded from position estimates or removed. Contact . Contact the operator of this site. Your node's ID (shown in the Meshtastic app, e.g. !a1b2c3d4) helps find its data. You can also lodge a complaint with your data protection authority; in Norway, that is Datatilsynet.

To stop your node's packets reaching public MQTT brokers at all, turn off "OK to MQTT" in its LoRa settings, and do not enable uplink on public brokers.
